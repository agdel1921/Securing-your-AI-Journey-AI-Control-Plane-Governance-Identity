AI is moving from answering to acting

The value is large. So is the question of trust.

Yesterday

AI that answers

Chat assistants and reports. A person reads the answer and decides.

Example: a summary of last night's yard congestion.

→
Today

AI that recommends

Tools suggest the next best action. A person still executes.

Example: a proposed berth plan for the planner to accept.

→
Next

AI agents that act

Software that plans, calls systems and completes tasks with little supervision.

Example: re-sequencing yard moves or releasing a customs hold.

Why ports are different

  • Always on. Operations run 24/7. An outage or a wrong action is felt within minutes.
  • Many countries. Each region has its own laws and regulators.
  • Safety and security. Cranes, vehicles, people and regulated cargo.
  • Many partners. Shipping lines, customs, hauliers, vendors and contractors.
  • Critical infrastructure. The port network matters to national supply chains.

The leadership question

Not “can we use AI?” but “can we trust AI to act on our behalf, and prove it to our Board, customers and regulators?”

Governance is what turns scattered pilots into confident, scaled operations.

Five questions every leader should be able to answer

A business framework, independent of any technology or supplier.

1

See

What AI agents do we have, and who owns them?

One live inventory across every terminal, region and vendor.

2

Identify

Who is each agent, and what may it touch?

Every agent has an identity, an owner and only the access it needs.

3

Decide

What may it do alone, and when must a person approve?

Clear policies and autonomy levels, enforced automatically.

4

Monitor

What is happening now, and can we step in?

One control plane with live visibility, alerts and a safe stop.

5

Prove

Can we show regulators and the Board we are in control?

Evidence collected as a by-product of operations.

Often missed in first-generation AI programmes

Agent identityAgents need their own identity, not a shared login or a borrowed staff account.
Autonomy tiersNot all AI deserves the same freedom. Match autonomy to impact.
Vendor-embedded AIAI arrives inside software you already bought. It must be in scope too.
ResilienceA tested fall-back to manual operations if an agent is paused or fails.
People and accountabilityA named owner for every agent, plus training and clear escalation.

      A practical path: start with visibility, scale with control

      Three horizons. Each one delivers value on its own.

      Four decisions for today

      1. Sponsor. Name one executive owner for AI risk and value.
      2. Baseline. Agree the autonomy tiers and what is never delegated.
      3. Visibility. Commission the group-wide agent inventory.
      4. Pilot. Choose two regions to prove the approach.

      How we would measure success

      • Share of agents with an owner and an identity
      • Time from idea to approved production use
      • Policy violations blocked before impact
      • Audit preparation effort and findings
      • Operational continuity during agent pause or failure
